The Concept

Two kitchens,
one lane.

There is a lane in every cook's memory — one that smells of sizzling garlic and soy, where the clatter of a wok echoes against stone walls hung with dried chili strings. There is another lane, softer, that carries the warm breath of slow-simmered tomatoes, of basil bruised by hand, of pasta rolled thin on a dusted wooden board. The Lombardy Lane is where those two lanes meet.

We are not a restaurant. We are a cloud kitchen — small, deliberate, and a little obsessive about what goes into every box we seal. Our kitchen does not do things at scale for the sake of scale. We do things at care.

"Every dish is an argument — a gentle one — that Chinese and Italian cooking were always meant to share a table."

The Chinese and Italian culinary traditions sit, surprisingly, very close together when you squint past their surfaces. Both worship garlic with religious fervour. Both believe that noodles, in whatever form, are the closest thing to comfort that flour and water can produce. Both use the wok's high heat and the pasta pot's rolling boil as instruments of transformation — raw things becoming something extraordinary in minutes. Both cultures have a saying, almost identical in spirit: cook with love, or don't cook at all.

At The Lombardy Lane, we hold that belief as our only non-negotiable. Our Italian Herb Dumplings are pleated with the same patience a Shanghainese grandmother would demand. Our Szechuan Pink Sauce Penne builds heat the way a Neapolitan sugo builds depth — slowly, layered, intentional. Nothing here is a shortcut. It is a conversation between two cuisines, happening one dish at a time.

The name? Lombardy — the northern Italian region that gave the world risotto, osso buco, and a sensibility that good food requires quiet confidence. Lane — because the best food in any city has always been found not on the boulevard, but just slightly off it, in a narrow passage that rewards curiosity. We are that lane. You found us.

We started with a simple question: what would happen if a Chinese kitchen's technique — its respect for heat, its love of contrasts, its umami depth — were applied to the Italian pantry? What if wonton wrappers held rosemary and fontina? What if spaghetti was finished in a wok with Sichuan peppercorn oil and a splash of balsamic? What if the ancient Chinese five-spice blend found itself tucked inside a ravioli pocket with chicken and feta?

The answer, it turns out, is extraordinarily delicious.

Every Dish, Its Story

What's on your plate
and why.

Every item on our menu was born from a genuine obsession. Here is the story behind each one — the inspiration, the technique, the tiny details you might not taste but will certainly feel.

🥟 Starter · Veg / NV
Italian Herb Dumplings
₹219 Veg / ₹249 Non-Veg

The dumpling is perhaps the most universal act of care in food. Across every culture that has folded dough around filling and applied heat, there is the same quiet gesture: someone took the time to make this for you.

We asked what would happen if the soul of a Chinese jiaozi — its delicate, translucent wrapper, its broth-soaked richness — were infused with the fragrant herb vocabulary of Italian cuisine. Rosemary. Thyme. Oregano. Pressed gently into the dough itself, so the wrapper is already flavoured before the filling is added.

Each piece is pleated by hand. Served eight to a portion with a simple broth that carries the herbs further, and a finishing crack of toasted black pepper and dried chili. Our house-made Signature Chili Oil Tomato Chutney — prepared in small batches, slow-infused — is the add-on we quietly recommend to everyone.

🧀 Starter · Veg
Mozzarella Tempura
₹229 Veg

Tempura is a Japanese gift to the world — a batter so light it barely exists, a shell that exists only to introduce texture before stepping aside. We borrow it here not from Japan but through the lens of Italian and Chinese thinking together: fresh, high-moisture mozzarella — the kind that weeps when you press it — coated and fried to a shattering, golden shell.

The dipping sauce is where the fusion lives: a soy balsamic vinegar reduction that is both salty and sweet, with the acidity of a good Italian vinegar and the umami depth of Chinese soy. The moment the hot cheese meets that cold, dark sauce, the two worlds stop being metaphorical.

🍝 Main · Non-Veg
Manchurian Meatball Pasta
₹289 Non-Veg

This dish is the one that started the whole idea. It was born from a very simple conversation: "What is the Chinese equivalent of a tomato meatball sauce?" The answer — arrived at after many failed attempts and one very successful one — is Manchurian.

The Italian meatball, with its fennel and parsley and Parmigiano, is rolled and seared in the same wok that builds the Manchurian sauce — a deep, tangy, fermented soy-ginger base that is arguably the soul of Indian-Chinese cooking. They are not natural companions. They are, once you try it, inseparable ones. Served over spaghetti. No apology needed.

🍜 Main · Veg / NV
Pomodoro Spice Noodles
₹289 Veg / ₹349 Non-Veg

The pomodoro — Italian for simply "tomato" — is the most humble and celebrated of sauces. Tomato, olive oil, garlic, salt. That is it. That restraint is what makes it extraordinary. We keep the restraint but then ask a single guest to enter: Chinese spice.

Dried chili. Star anise. White pepper. A whisper of five-spice. They do not overwhelm the tomato — they amplify it, the way a bass note amplifies a melody. Served over stir-fried noodles so the sauce can coat every strand and cling the way a good pomodoro should. A finishing chili thread for heat. A simple dish that is far harder to get right than it looks.

🥜 Main · Veg / NV
Kung Pao Penne
₹269 Veg / ₹299 Non-Veg

Kung Pao is one of the great Chinese dishes — ancient in origin, brilliant in construction. It is a study in textural and flavour contrast: the soft protein, the crunch of peanut, the numbing heat of dried chili, the sweetness of a well-made sauce.

We preserve every one of those contrasts but change the vehicle. Penne — with its ridges and its hollow core — catches the balsamic-soy reduction in a way no other pasta does. Roasted peanuts give the crunch. Blackened Sichuan chilies give the heat. The balsamic's acidity softens everything into a sauce that is simultaneously familiar and completely new. One of our most-loved dishes by people who were not expecting to love it.

🫕 Main · Non-Veg
Five Spiced Ravioli
₹339 Non-Veg

Ravioli is a labour of love. You make the dough. You rest it. You roll it. You fill it, seal it, ensure it doesn't tear when it meets boiling water. This is exactly the kind of thing a cloud kitchen is supposed to save time on by not doing. We do it anyway.

The filling — chicken, feta, fresh basil — is Italian in spirit. The sauce — roasted tomato with garlic, bathed in Chinese five spice — is where the dish shifts hemispheres. Five spice (star anise, cloves, cinnamon, Sichuan pepper, fennel seeds) has a natural kinship with slow-roasted tomato that most people have simply never thought to explore. We have. Repeatedly. We are confident you will not want to go back.

🍄 Main · Veg / NV
Sichuan Boscaiola
₹339 Veg / ₹389 Non-Veg

Boscaiola means "of the woods" in Italian — a creamy, earthy pasta traditionally built on mushrooms, bacon or sausage, and a rich white sauce. It is an autumn dish. It tastes like the inside of a warm room while rain falls outside.

We keep the woods. We keep the cream and the mushrooms. But we plant a Sichuan peppercorn right at the heart of it, and it changes everything. Sichuan pepper does not burn. It tingles — a citrusy, electric sensation on the tongue that food scientists have a name for (mala) and that everyone who tries it has a different emotional response to. Finished with chili oil and melted cheese. One of those dishes that quietly becomes a person's favourite.

🌶️ Main · Veg / NV
Lao Gan Ma Aglio E Olio
₹309 Veg / ₹349 Non-Veg

Aglio e Olio is the pasta you make at midnight when you have nothing in the fridge. Garlic. Olive oil. Pasta water. Chili. That is it. And it is one of the most profound things Italian cuisine has ever produced — proof that simplicity and greatness are not opposites.

Lao Gan Ma is China's most beloved condiment — a jar of crunchy, fermented chili crisp with fried garlic, black bean, and a depth of umami so concentrated it makes other condiments feel apologetic. Combine these two garlic-loving, chili-respecting, oil-carrying traditions and the result is louder, crunchier, more electric than either parent. We finish with a poached egg and a hit of lemon zest — the egg for richness, the lemon to cut through everything and remind you what subtlety feels like.

🌸 Signature
Szechuan Pink Sauce Penne
₹349 Veg / ₹389 Non-Veg

The pink sauce — rosé, as the Italians call it — is cream and tomato in conversation. Neither dominant, both necessary. It is a sauce of diplomacy. We respected that and then introduced a Szechuan agitator into the diplomatic conversation.

Our house-made Szechuan chili crisp is prepared in small batches — slow-fried garlic, dried Sichuan chilies, toasted peppercorns, a breath of five-spice. It is stirred through the pink sauce so that every bite is creamy and gentle right up until it isn't. The warmth blooms after. It is one of those sauces that makes you eat faster without realising it. Our most reordered dish.

🌿 Signature
Wok Fired Pesto Jasmine Rice
₹349 Non-Veg

Pesto does not belong in a wok. That is, of course, exactly why we put it there. The wok's fierce heat does something to basil pesto that a gentle toss cannot — it toasts the pine nuts and garlic further, causes the edges to char very slightly, and releases a smoky, grassy compound that smells like a garden in August and tastes like something you can't quite name but instantly love.

Jasmine rice was chosen for its fragrance — it carries aromatics in a way that basmati doesn't, and it has just enough stickiness to absorb the wok's residual flavours. Shredded chicken, Chinese spice, and crispy garlic arrive just before plating. The result is three food cultures — Italian, Chinese, South-East Asian — colliding briefly and coming out as something entirely their own.

🐉 Signature
Dragon Lasagna Cupcakes
₹309 Veg / ₹359 Non-Veg

Lasagna is, at its core, a layered argument. Sheet after sheet of pasta, each separated by sauce and cheese and protein, building a structure that is greater than the sum of its parts. We love that logic. We changed the building material.

Wonton wrappers — thin, slightly eggy, with a satisfying snap when fried — replace lasagna sheets. Baked in a cupcake tin so each one is its own self-contained tower. The filling: ginger-garlic soy mince with Italian herbs, melted cheese, and Sichuan spice threaded through everything. The result looks like a little individual portion of something artful and tastes like a very happy argument between a Chinese kitchen and an Italian one — with no loser. The dragon in the name is earned.
